If you have a dataset that could be useful to the community, we would like to hear about it,
and the earlier the better.

There is no upload form. Datasets vary a lot in size, format and licensing, so we start with
an email and then arrange a transfer to suit what you have.

## Start by emailing us

Write to [data@virtualflybrain.org](mailto:data@virtualflybrain.org?subject=Submitting%20Image%20data).
A short description is enough to get the conversation going, but the following will save a
round trip:

* What the data is: expression patterns, single neurons, lineage clones, an EM reconstruction,
  a new template, or something else.
* Roughly how many images, and the approximate total size.
* How it was imaged (confocal, light-sheet, EM) and at what resolution.
* Whether it is registered, and if so [to which template](/docs/data/templates/). If it is not
  registered yet, say so. See
  [registering your images](/docs/contribution-guidelines/image-registration/), and ask us
  before you start if you are unsure which template to target.
* The associated paper or preprint, with a DOI if there is one.
* The licence, or what terms you want the data released under. We need terms that allow us to
  redistribute the images through VFB.
* Who to contact about the dataset.

{{< alert title="Use data@, not support@" color="warning" >}}
`data@virtualflybrain.org` is a private address that reaches the VFB curation team.
`support@virtualflybrain.org` goes to a
[publicly archived Google Group](https://groups.google.com/g/vfb-suport). Please don't send
unpublished or embargoed data, or anything you would not want indexed, to that address.
{{< /alert >}}

## What happens next

We will reply with advice on whether the data is a good fit and what we need alongside it, and
set up a transfer route sized to the dataset. Small sets can go by a shared link; for larger
ones we will arrange something separately.

Curation then brings the images into VFB's coordinate space and ontology so they can be found,
overlaid and compared with everything else on the site. We record and display the provenance
of every image, so your dataset stays attributed to you and to the paper it came from.

## Contact us early

The best time to get in touch is while you are still planning the experiment. Choices made at
the bench affect whether an image can be registered at all: which counterstain you use, what
voxel size you image at, whether the whole brain is in the frame. These are all awkward to
revisit once the data exists.

If you have already generated a dataset we can still help. Send us what you have and we will
tell you what is possible with it.
