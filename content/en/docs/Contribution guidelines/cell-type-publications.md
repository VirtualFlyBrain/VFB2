We are always interested in published work that describes cell types in *Drosophila*, and we
want to capture it in the [Drosophila Anatomy Ontology](https://github.com/FlyBase/drosophila-anatomy-developmental-ontology)
(DAO) that powers Virtual Fly Brain. Two kinds of paper are especially useful:

* **New characterisation of cell types** — a paper that describes a cell type for the first
  time, refines its definition, or adds anatomical, connectivity, developmental or expression
  detail that would improve how we describe it.
* **Synonym mapping** — a paper that shows two named cell types are the same thing, gives a new
  name for a known type, or otherwise reconciles the naming used across studies. This is one of
  the most valuable things you can tell us, because it lets us link data that would otherwise
  stay separate.

You do not have to be an author of the paper. If you have read something that we should know
about, please pass it on.

## How to let us know

There are two ways, and either is fine. Whichever you use, the more precise you can be about
*which* cell types the paper covers, the faster we can act on it.

### Open an issue on the ontology repo (preferred)

Raise a ticket on the DAO issue tracker:

* [FlyBase/drosophila-anatomy-developmental-ontology issues](https://github.com/FlyBase/drosophila-anatomy-developmental-ontology/issues/new)

An issue is the best route because it stays with the ontology, is publicly visible, and lets us
track the work and discuss it with you if we have questions.

### Email us

If you would rather not use GitHub, email
[support@virtualflybrain.org](mailto:support@virtualflybrain.org?subject=Publication%20with%20cell%20type%20information).
This address goes to a [publicly archived support forum](https://groups.google.com/g/vfb-suport),
which is fine for pointing us at published work.

## What to include

A few details make the difference between a note we can act on straight away and one that needs
a round of questions:

* **The reference** — a DOI, PubMed ID, or FlyBase reference (FBrf) if you have one.
* **Which cell types** — the names used in the paper, and, where you know them, the matching
  VFB or ontology terms.
* **What is new** — is this a first description, added detail, or a synonym/renaming? For a
  synonym, say which names refer to the same type.
* **Where in the paper** — a figure or section number that pins down the relevant part is very
  helpful.

You do not need all of this to get in touch. Even a bare reference with a line on why it matters
is worth sending.
