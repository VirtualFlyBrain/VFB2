BBC News has published a feature, ['How a fly brain may help explain the secrets of human behaviour'](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqjkd2ylq1yo), on the first detailed comparison of the brains of male and female fruit flies.

{{< youtube id="CeChQSyvzKE" autoplay="true" >}}

The comparison comes from the new connectome of the complete male *Drosophila* central nervous system: every neuron and connection in the brain and the ventral nerve cord of one animal, published in *Cell* by a team led from the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology, HHMI Janelia Research Campus, the University of Cambridge, Google Research and the Champalimaud Foundation, with Greg Jefferis, one of VFB's Principal Investigators, as senior author. Set against the existing female connectomes, it shows that most cell types are shared between the sexes, and picks out the smaller set that differ.

The BBC piece follows those differences into behaviour. Speaking to co-author Philipp Schlegel, it describes the wiring found only in males that lets them court a female by vibrating their wings to sing to her, and the circuits behind male aggression. Geneticists have known for decades that genes set up these sex differences in behaviour; what the connectome adds is the wiring those genes produce. That link from genes to circuits to behaviour is why Greg Jefferis expects the work to help explain how brains differ in human conditions with a strong genetic basis, such as autism and schizophrenia.

The male CNS connectome is on Virtual Fly Brain alongside FlyWire, the hemibrain, MANC, the optic lobe dataset and BANC, all registered to the same template brains and named with one anatomy vocabulary. That makes the comparison the BBC describes something anyone can make: look up a neuron type and see which datasets its members turn up in, and how their connections compare. HHMI Janelia's visualisations of the dataset are in our news posts on [the male CNS connectome](/blog/2026/09/03/video-the-male-fly-cns-connectome-visualised/) and [the first complete map of a male fly's central nervous system](/blog/2026/09/03/video-the-first-complete-map-of-a-male-flys-entire-central-nervous-system/).
